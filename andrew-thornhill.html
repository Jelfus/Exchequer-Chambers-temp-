Overview

Andrew Thornhill KC is a highly experienced barrister with a distinguished practice spanning tax law and related areas. Andrew was a member of Pump Court Tax Chambers for over 50 years and was Head of Chambers for 20 years, during which he increased membership from 8 to 39. Andrew has appeared at all levels of courts.

He has extensive expertise across the full spectrum of tax law, with particular emphasis on employee benefit trusts, employee remuneration, share schemes, inheritance tax and capital gains tax planning, and tax litigation. His advice is sought after both for preventative planning and for the resolution of contentious disputes.

Alongside his tax practice, Andrew served for over a decade as a Recorder (Criminal) on the Western Circuit. This judicial experience has equipped him with a rare depth of understanding of evidential issues, advocacy and the conduct of proceedings at the highest level.

Andrew has experience of being instructed to conduct large-scale independent investigations, often in sensitive contexts where wrongdoing must be uncovered. He has considerable expertise in tracing the flow of funds, uncovering hidden assets and establishing accountability within organisations and corporate structures.

Current areas of work

Andrew is currently heavily engaged in two areas: exits from film schemes and contractors’ appeals regarding the loan charge. Over the last 15 years or so, HMRC has succeeded in winning cases that establish film leasing not to be a trade but an investment. In many instances, taxpayers who successfully claimed deductions for film expenditure on the basis that they carried on a film leasing trade are free to assert that they are not trading. In that case, the exit charges for those leaving film leasing would not apply. This has been confirmed by the decision in the Hoyle case. HMRC have appealed.

As regards the loan charge, and open years where HMRC claim tax on earnings in relation to contractor schemes, there are several possible challenges to liability. One line of defence rests on European law. The point was approached in the Finucane case in Scotland, but Lord Tyre held the matter had to be heard in the Tax Tribunal.

A second line of defence rests on tax credits. The argument is that the original receipt of the loan gave rise to earnings in respect of which the contractor obtains a tax credit. The tax credit enables him to argue that he has paid the tax and that this payment reduces the loan charge to nil.

The Loan Charge Action Group proposes to bring a test case from which any taxpayer with a loan charge can benefit on joining the group. Andrew is also exploring new ways of using life policies to plan for inheritance tax and ways in which pension schemes can invest in residential property. He is happy to consider new ideas and develop them with clients.
